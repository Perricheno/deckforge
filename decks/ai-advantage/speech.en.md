# Speech: AI advantage (about 5 minutes)

## Slide 1
Our topic is the source of competitive advantage in the age of AI. The research question is whether the advantage is produced by AI technology itself, or by complementary capabilities: leadership, workflows, talent, culture and governance. We argue that AI is necessary but not sufficient.

## Slide 2
There are three reasons why AI technology alone cannot create a lasting advantage. First, models are converging: the quality of leading providers is becoming similar. Second, costs are falling, so the same capability becomes cheaper for every competitor. Third, access is open: rivals can obtain the same tools within weeks. A resource must be valuable, rare and difficult to copy to give a lasting advantage. AI is valuable, but it is neither rare nor difficult to copy.

## Slide 3
There are two reasons why complements matter. First, a new technology brings gains only after companies invest in processes, skills and organization. The J-curve shows this: productivity usually falls first and then rises. Second, history repeats this pattern. Factories gained from electricity only after they redesigned production, and computers followed the same path. For generative AI, we treat this as a hypothesis.

## Slide 4
Our framework is based on three propositions. First, access to AI alone does not give an advantage, because it is not rare. Second, the value of AI increases with the strength of the complements. The relationship is multiplicative, not additive: if the complements are weak, the result is close to zero. Third, complements are specific to each company and develop slowly, which makes them difficult to imitate.

## Slide 5
Each of the five capabilities contributes in a different way. Leadership sets priorities and helps pilots reach full scale. Workflows turn the technology into real gains in speed, quality and cost. Talent combines knowledge of the field with AI skills. Culture supports experimentation and trust in data. Governance provides rules for data, risk and responsibility. All five take years to build, so competitors cannot simply buy them.

## Slide 6
Our illustration shows two firms that use the same model at the same cost. Firm A runs pilots without changing its processes and captures about 22% of the potential value. Firm B redesigns its processes, trains its staff and introduces governance, and captures about 86%. These figures are hypothetical and only illustrate the logic. In conclusion, AI is necessary but not sufficient, because durable advantage comes from complementary capabilities. Therefore, companies should invest in processes, training and governance, not only in licences, and leaders should be accountable for the results of adoption.
